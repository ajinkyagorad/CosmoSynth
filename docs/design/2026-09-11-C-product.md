## 1. The product in one page

CosmoSynth 2.0 is one Quest 3 mixed-reality app. It opens in passthrough, with the real Earth
in front of you as it is right now. From there one continuous scale control takes you up a
ladder of 19 rungs: the sky over your city, low orbit, the Earth and Moon, the planets, the
heliosphere, the Oort cloud, the nearest stars, the local clouds, the Milky Way, its halo, the
Local Group, the Local Volume, Laniakea, the cosmic web, and finally the observable universe
wrapped in the cosmic microwave background. Every rung shows only real, cited data at real
positions, and every object can be picked, inspected, measured and flown to.

Beside the real universe sit two clearly different kinds of content:

- **Phenomena**: nine exhibits (black hole, merger, lensing, galaxy encounter, cosmic web, dark
  matter halo, stellar interior, reconnection, Alfven waves), each a small model on a plinth, each
  reachable from the real object it explains (the Sun, Sgr A*, M31, the Milky Way, galaxy
  clusters) and each stating what it is and is not.
- **Fictional sectors**: the user's holographic star map, and generated systems, shown only as
  holograms on a neon projector. That look is never used for anything real.

Time runs forwards and backwards from 1 Myr per second the other way to 1 Myr per second
forwards; any date can be reached, and real missions (Voyager, Cassini, New Horizons,
BepiColombo, Artemis I, Apollo 11 and the other Horizons spacecraft) can be played back
against the real planets. Tools measure anything to anything, show the sky from any vantage to
any limiting magnitude, launch probes through the real Solar System, hold planets side by side,
fade the room out into full space, and make the universe audible.

What makes it CosmoSynth and not a planetarium clone is the rule it already had: every value
says how it is known (Measured, Inferred, Procedural, Fictional), and every presentation lie
(sizes, brightness, magnification) is named on screen while it is active.

## 2. The journey: the scale ladder

### 2.1 Units and the one scale limit

The app's scale variable is `FloatingOrigin.UnitsPerKm`: room metres per real kilometre
(1 render unit = 1 m). All ladder values below are UnitsPerKm, written `U`, with `log10 U`
alongside. For cross-reference with the Milky Way Atlas's metres-per-parsec slider,
`atlas log(m/pc) = log10 U + 13.489` (INFERRED arithmetic, [MWA] §4.1).

```csharp
// Core/Presentation/ScaleLimits.cs  (engine-free)
public static class ScaleLimits
{
    public const double MinUnitsPerKm = 2e-25; // observable universe (8.8e23 km) = 0.176 m
    public const double MaxUnitsPerKm = 1e3;   // true scale: 1 m of space = 1 m of room
    public static double Clamp(double unitsPerKm) =>
        Math.Min(MaxUnitsPerKm, Math.Max(MinUnitsPerKm, unitsPerKm));
    public static bool AtLimit(double unitsPerKm, out bool atMax);   // for the haptic bump + readout
}
```

Why these two numbers:

- **Max 1e3.** CosmoSynth never needs to magnify space. The smallest objects anyone frames are
  landers (~3 m) and they sit comfortably below 1:1 at hand size (0.4 m / 0.003 km = 133). Today's
  `TabletopManipulator` ceiling is 1e6 (VERIFIED [ARC] §2.7), which allows 1 m of space to fill
  1 km of room and serves nothing. Capping at 1:1 turns "true scale" into a physical fact the
  user can feel: the top of the ladder *is* reality.
- **Min 2e-25.** The CMB rung puts the observable universe (radius ~4.4e23 km, the orchestrator's
  figure; [DAT] §3.5 derives 13.87 Gpc = 4.28e23 km) at 1.2 m across. The limit lets the user
  pull it down to a palm-sized 17.6 cm ball without allowing a scale at which it vanishes.
  Today's floor is 1e-20 (VERIFIED [ARC] §5.13), five decades short.
- **Range: 27.7 decades.** Float safety of layer transforms at the floor is the Foundation
  section's to confirm; [ARC] §5.6 already argues `localScale` is fine to ~1e-24 x Mpc units
  (INFERRED).

At a limit the scale stops, the controller pulses once (0.5, 30 ms), and the header readout
flashes "limit: true scale (1:1)" or "limit: observable universe" for 1.5 s. No other clamp may
exist anywhere in the input path (the atlas's two-hand clamp at 10^0.5 m/pc snapped the view
out of the Solar System, [MWA] B1; CosmoSynth's 1.4.0 complaint "restrictive to solar system
distance" was the same class, VERIFIED `docs/feedback.md`).

room-size planet, 1:1 for the two Earth vantages.

0.05 decades of it and framed on its centre). Continuous zoom updates the highlight; it never
snaps to a rung.

### 2.3 The room-level plane

Which celestial plane lies flat in the room is a presentation rotation of the universe anchor
(the Universe host's rotation in the room). It is separate from, and applied after, the one
ICRF->Unity mapping inside `FloatingOrigin` (binding decision 4): the mapping is fixed; the
plane is a choice.

```csharp
// Core/Presentation/PresentationPlane.cs
public enum PresentationPlane { Horizon, LocalVertical, FocusEquator, Equatorial, Ecliptic, Galactic, Supergalactic }
// PlaneUp(plane, context) -> ICRF unit vector that is placed on room-up:
//   Horizon       local zenith at the site at time t (rotates with Earth)
//   LocalVertical radial from Earth's centre through the viewer
//   FocusEquator  focus body's IAU north pole at t (BodyOrientation.PoleDirectionAt)
//   Equatorial    ICRF +z
//   Ecliptic      ecliptic north pole, J2000, obliquity 84381.448" (StandardFrames.EquatorialToEcliptic)
//   Galactic      NGP, RA 192.85948 deg, Dec 27.12825 deg (StandardFrames.EquatorialToGalactic)
//   Supergalactic SGP at (l, b) = (47.37 deg, +6.32 deg) ([DAT] §9.2 matrix)
```

Rules:

1. **The plane changes only on a rung tap, a GO, or the PLANE control.** Continuous zoom, flight
   and grabs never tilt the universe (the atlas report's warning that rotating the world while
   zooming disorients, [MWA] §6 item 1, INFERRED but adopted).
2. The rotation that levels a plane is the minimal one that takes the old up-vector to the new
   one, followed by the yaw that keeps the travel target in front of the user. It is animated as
   part of the travel (§6.1). In Space mode (room hidden, §8.5) a tilt larger than 10 deg is not
   animated: the view blinks (0.15 s fade out, snap, 0.15 s fade in) instead, because a rolling
   starfield with no room reference induces vection.
3. `PLANE: Auto` (default) uses the rung's plane on a rung tap, and on a GO the plane of the rung
   nearest in log10 U to the arrival scale; stars keep the current plane. Choosing any fixed
   plane in TOOLS -> PLANE pins it until Auto is chosen again.
4. When zooming has left the user nearer (in log10 U) to a rung whose plane differs from the
   active one, a small chip appears in the panel header and on the watch face: "Level to
   Galactic". Tapping it levels; ignoring it is fine. It never levels by itself.
5. **Horizon** puts *north* behind the user at the moment of arrival, so a northern-hemisphere
   user faces south, where the ecliptic and planets are. The real room's compass heading is
   unknown (Quest has no compass, INFERRED), so the compass ring at the horizon says N/E/S/W and
   the user turns with snap turn or a two-hand twist.

### 2.5 Moving up and down the ladder

Three inputs change scale, all through `ScaleLimits.Clamp` and all with the same pivot rule.

1. **Two-hand gesture** (both grips, or both pinches): keep today's `TabletopManipulator` law,
   scale factor `(span / span0)^2`, log-space smoothing `1 - 0.5^(60 dt)` (VERIFIED [ARC] §2.7),
   plus the atlas's **yaw-only twist** from the horizontal inter-hand vector ([MWA] §4.19). The
   manipulator's target is cleared when reached, so it no longer fights rungs and travel
   ([ARC] B5). Hand points are converted with `Universe.InverseTransformPoint` first ([ARC] B4).
2. **Right stick Y** (controllers; mirrored for left-handed): zoom at **0.6 decades per second**
   at full deflection, deadzone 0.12, rate `0.6 · sign(y) · ((|y| - 0.12) / 0.88)^1.5`, times the
   boost factor from the left trigger (x1 ... x4). 27.7 decades take 46 s unboosted and 11.5 s
   boosted. **Mouse wheel** on desktop: 0.1 decade per notch.
3. **Rung tap** in GO (or `rung:<id>` in the harness): a `Travel` (§6.1) to the rung's framing.

**Pivot rule** (one function, `ZoomPivot`): (a) the followed object if Follow is on; else (b)
the selected object if it is drawn (`IsGeometryDrawn` or a visible layer point); else, for the
two-hand gesture only, (c) the hands' midpoint if any drawn object lies within 0.6 m of it
(today's rule, VERIFIED [ARC] §2.7); else (d) the anchor, i.e. the tabletop centre. The pivot's
absolute km position is held fixed in the room while U changes.

**Flight** (left stick) moves the viewer through the universe at
`ScaleSpace.NavigationSpeed(distance to nearest object of any layer)`, so the same deflection
crosses the visible scene in a few seconds at every rung. Two changes: the direction is the full
head-forward vector (so looking up and pushing forward rises; no separate rise axis), and the
nearest-object query includes layer points, not only `CelestialBodyView`s ([ARC] §2.7 note).
The speed ceiling `1e14 km/s` must rise to at least `1e24 km/s` (Foundation/Runtime to set;
[ARC] §5.13 derives 1e23 for a 4 s crossing, INFERRED).

**Cancelling:** any grab, stick zoom, stick flight, rung tap or GO cancels a travel in progress
([MWA] B12). A zoom never changes the plane (§2.3).

**Readout** (panel header and watch face): the rung name, then `1 m = <distance>` using the unit
ladder m, km, AU, ly, kpc, Mpc, Gpc with 3 significant figures, e.g. `Whole Solar System · 1 m =
88.6 AU`. The watch face shows the log10 U position as a tick on a 27.7-decade arc with the five
group names, so the user always sees where on the ladder they are.

### 2.6 How honest the distances are

Binding decision 3 compresses far content per group about the eye (Foundation owns the formula).
The product consequences, which the UI states in INFO -> THIS VIEW rather than as a badge:

- **Angular sizes and directions are exact**, and so is front-to-back order inside a group. What
  a far group loses is stereo depth beyond the compression knee. A real observer cannot perceive
  stereo depth beyond ~76 m either ([QRT] §3.4, INFERRED), so no badge is shown for it.
- **Every number is true.** The inspector, the measure tool, the scale readout and the travel
  planner all use absolute double km, never render positions.
- The only presentation departures that get a badge are the ones that change what the eye reads
  as size or brightness: sizes (§8.6), brightness assists (§4.3), and presentation gains such as

## 3. Modes: one universe, three kinds of truth

### 3.1 The stage contract

The room holds **one active stage** at a time. Switching stages fades the old one out and the new
one in over 0.4 s at the same anchor; the stages that are not active keep their state and resume
exactly where they were.

|---|---|---|---|

The global clock keeps running while another stage is active. Real and invented content are
never in the room at the same time, which is the whole point: two scales, two clocks and two grab
targets side by side would make every gesture ambiguous and would invite the reading that an
exhibit is to scale with the real Sun beside it.

A Core test enforces the boundary (mirroring the Phase 2 test): **no field reachable from the
real catalogue is Fictional, no field of a Fictional catalogue is anything but Fictional or
Procedural, and no Fictional body carries an ICRF `PositionKm`.**

## 4. Layers

### 4.1 One table drives everything

```csharp
// Core/Layers/LayerTable.cs  (engine-free; the only list of layers and rungs:
//   public static readonly LayerDef[] Layers; public static readonly Rung[] Rungs;  // Rungs = §2.2)
public enum LayerGroup { SolarSystem, Stars, DustAndNebulae, Galaxies, Beyond, Guides }
public enum SoloGroup  { None, Solar, Stars, Dust, Galaxies, Beyond }   // None = unaffected by SOLO
public sealed record LayerDef(
    string Id, string Caption, LayerGroup Group, SoloGroup Solo, bool DefaultOn,
    double MinLog10U, double MaxLog10U,        // visibility window; ±0.25-decade linear fade inside each end
    LabelCategory? Labels,                      // category its objects label under, or null
    string[] DataKinds,                         // manifest.json "kind"s it needs (Data section §3); empty = Core-only
    ProvenanceClass Weakest, string ProvenanceNote,
    LayerParam[] Params);                       // sliders/choices the panel generates
public sealed record LayerParam(string Id, string Caption, double Min, double Max, double Default,
    string Unit, bool IsPresentationGain);      // gain != Default raises a header chip (§4.4)
```

`LayerState` (Core) holds on/off, SOLO and param values and answers one pure question per layer:

```
Weight(id, log10U, ctx) = on(id) · soloAllows(id) · window(id, log10U) · available(id, ctx)
    window = clamp01((log10U - Min) / 0.25) · clamp01((Max - log10U) / 0.25)
    available = every DataKind listed in manifest.json and loaded (LayerReady) ∧ layer-specific condition (ctx)
```

Licences need no runtime switch: the Data section's licence profile decides at bake time which
files ship, and the runtime loads only what `manifest.json` lists (Data section §1.5, §3). A kind
absent from the manifest makes the row read `not in this build` (with the manifest's reason when it
records one); a kind listed but failing to load reads `missing` or `invalid`. Where the profile
wrote a fallback to the same path (WMAP for Planck, Edenhofer for Vergely, SDSS-only for 2MRS,
hand-entered giants for UNGC), the layer runs and its legend names the shipped source from the
manifest's attribution.

The renderer multiplies its alpha by `Weight`; a layer with `Weight = 0` is not drawn and not
pickable. The LAYERS tab, the desktop HUD replacement, the legend, the harness switches
(`layer:<id>:on|off`, `solo:<group>`) and INFO -> THIS VIEW are all generated from the table.
**Tests** (headless): ids unique; every `Min < Max` within `ScaleLimits`; every rung in §2.2 has at
least one default-on layer with `Weight > 0` at its U; every `SoloGroup` except None has at least
one layer; every `DataKind` is a kind in the Data section's file table; `Weight` fades continuously (no step
larger than 0.1 between log10U samples 0.01 apart).

## 6. Navigation

### 6.1 `Travel(target, framing)`

Today there are three unrelated travel mechanisms (dashboard teleport + `FrameBodyAtSize`,
desktop `UniverseNavigator.TravelTo`, harness `FlyToStar`; VERIFIED [ARC] §5.11). 2.0 has one:
the Runtime `TravelController` plays a Core `TravelPlan` and writes `FloatingOrigin.OriginKm`,
`UnitsPerKm` (through `ScaleLimits`), the anchor yaw and the presentation plane. The dashboard,
the selector, the footer's GO, the A button, double-click, the harness and mission playback all
call it.

```csharp
// Core/Nav/TravelPlan.cs  (engine-free)
public readonly record struct ViewState(Double3 FocusKm, double Log10U, double YawRad, Double3 PlaneUpIcrf);
public sealed class TravelPlan
{
    public static TravelPlan Create(ViewState from, ViewState to, double fitSpanMetres = 0.6);
    public double DurationSeconds { get; }
    public ViewState At(double seconds, Double3 currentTargetKm);   // target re-read each frame: moving bodies
    public bool UsesDip { get; }
}
```

Profile, with `u = t / T` and `s(x) = x^3 (x (6x - 15) + 10)` (smootherstep):

1. **Orientation first.** `er = s(min(1, u / 0.35))`; yaw = angle-lerp(yaw0, yaw1, er), where
   yaw1 turns the target to straight ahead of the user; plane up = slerp(up0, up1, er). Both are
   complete at u = 0.35 (the atlas's yaw-first, [MWA] §4.16).
2. **Direct profile** when start and target already fit the room together at the smaller
   scale, i.e. when `Lfit = log10(fitSpan / D) >= min(l0, l1)` with `D = |f1 - f0|` km:
   `e = s(u)`, `log10U = lerp(l0, l1, e)`, `focus = lerp(f0, f1, e)`. Interpolating the focus in
   absolute km (not the group position) keeps the target on the line from the anchor for the
   whole flight ([MWA] §4.16).
3. **Dip profile** otherwise (Earth to M31, say): the scale first falls to `Lfit`, where both
   ends are within ±0.3 m of the anchor, and the focus moves only while zoomed out:
   `log10U = lerp(l0, Lfit, s(2u))` for u <= 0.5 and `lerp(Lfit, l1, s(2u - 1))` after;
   `focus = lerp(f0, f1, s(clamp((u - 0.25) / 0.5, 0, 1)))`. Without it, a straight log-lerp
   leaves both ends millions of room metres away mid-flight and the user sees nothing (INFERRED
   arithmetic: Earth to M31 at the midpoint scale puts M31 ~1.2e7 m away). This is a cheap
   version of van Wijk and Nuij's optimal zoom-pan; the full method is not needed.
4. **Duration** `T = min(2.6 + 0.35 · decades, 9.0)` s, with decades = `|l1 - l0|` (direct) or
   `|l0 - Lfit| + |l1 - Lfit|` (dip). Examples (INFERRED arithmetic): Local clouds to Whole Solar
   System 4.9 s (direct); Earth in hand to M31 8.3 s (dip); Sky from Earth to the CMB 9 s (the
   cap; 12 s uncapped).
5. **Cancel** on any grab, stick zoom, stick flight, rung tap or new GO; the view stays where it
   is. The manipulator's stale target is cleared.
6. **Arrival:** the crosshair (8 strokes along the cube diagonals from 0.55 R to R, R = 7.5 cm at
   the target, Accent, [MWA] §4.16) appears **at arrival** and fades after 6 s over 1.2 s (the atlas
   started the timer at GO, [MWA] B11). Follow switches on for moving targets (§6.2). During the
   flight a caption under the panel header names each rung passed ("Inner planets · Whole Solar
   System · Heliosphere ..."): the powers-of-ten moment, for the cost of one text line.

**Tests:** endpoints exact; zero velocity of log10U and focus at u = 0 and 1 (finite difference);
yaw and plane complete at u = 0.35; direct profile: the target direction from the anchor
constant to 1e-9 rad; dip profile: both ends within ±fitSpan/2 of the anchor at u = 0.5, and
log10U monotonic in each half; T <= 9 s; a plan evaluated past T returns `to`.

### 6.2 Follow and Free

- **Follow** (co-moving, translation only): every frame `OriginKm = target.AbsolutePositionKm(now)
  + offsetKm`. The offset is re-captured when a grab or a flight ends (V2's `followOff`, [HEL]
  §7.2), so the user can move around a followed planet. Without it, at 1 day per second Earth
  leaves a tabletop hologram in about a second (30 km/s x 86,400 s = 2.6e6 km per real second,
  VERIFIED arithmetic in [HEL] §7.2).
- On by default after GO to a body, spacecraft or star system, and after rungs 1-9; off after
  rungs 10-18 and GO to fixed targets (clusters, galaxies, structures).

### 6.5 Comfort rules

1. **In passthrough the room never moves**; only the stage moves. The room is the user's balance
   reference, which is why passthrough is the default.
2. **In Space mode** (room hidden): travel yaw at most 60 deg/s; plane changes over 10 deg are a
   blink, not a rotation (§2.3); the **comfort floor** (a faint 1.5 m ring at the user's feet,
   TOOLS -> ROOM) is on by default.
3. No roll or pitch of the stage except a plane change and the opt-in **Free tilt** (one-hand grab
   carries full 6-DoF rotation, as the atlas's grab did, [MWA] §4.19; default off).
4. Snap turn by default; smooth turn opt-in.
5. Flight and zoom accelerate over 0.25 s and stop over 0.15 s; nothing moves the view without
   a user action except Follow, mission playback and a travel the user started.
6. No full-view flashes: stage switches and passthrough changes fade over at least 0.3 s;
   luminance never changes by more than 30 % of the view within 100 ms.
7. Content nearer than 0.25 m to the eyes fades out (vergence strain); rungs frame their centre
   at least 0.45 m away.
8. No head-locked content: the panel, cards and badges are world- or wrist-locked.
9. The frame rate never drops below 72 Hz by design: the quality governor sheds dust steps, then
   star vertices, then galaxy sprites, and says so (`QUALITY REDUCED` chip).

### 8.2 Observed sky

- TOOLS -> OBSERVED SKY switches the `stars` layer (and Cepheids, globulars) from **model** mode

  by its apparent magnitude from a **vantage**, `m = M + 5 log10(D / 10 pc) + A_V`, visible when

  eye response `b^0.23` ([MWA] §4.4). The maths lives in Core `Photometry` with tests (Sirius

- **Vantage:** Sun (default) · Me (the viewer's absolute position) · Selected (any star or body) ·
  Site (a place on Earth; rung 0 uses it). The atlas used the camera's model-space position, which
  at tabletop scale is kiloparsecs outside the data ([MWA] B9); the explicit choice fixes that.
- **Extinction** on/off (needs `dust`): adds `A_V` along vantage -> star from the dust cube.
- **SITE:** a list of 12 cities (Helsinki default: 60.17 N, 24.94 E; plus Sydney, Santiago,

- The readout says what the data can show: "8,920 stars brighter than 6.5 from the Sun" and, when

### 8.3 Trajectory sandbox (Astro-Volume and the Orbital Slingshot)

**Real sandbox** (TOOLS -> SANDBOX -> Launch from selected; default Earth):

- Core `RestrictedNBody`: a massless probe in the field of the catalogue bodies whose positions
  come from the real ephemerides (what `orbital_simulator.html` really is, [HEL] §7.7), adaptive
  Dormand-Prince 5(4) in double, with events for surface impact (the body's ellipsoid) and
  sphere-of-influence changes. **The predicted path is the same integrator and the same steps as
  the flight**, so what is drawn is what is flown (fixes the slingshot's divergence, [HEL] bug 13).
- **Aiming:** pinch (or trigger) near the launch body, pull back, release, the slingshot gesture
  with its 22 mm / 45 mm hysteresis and release buffer (re-use the last non-zero impulse if the
  release frame reads zero, [HEL] §3.4). The pull length maps logarithmically to Δv over
  0.1-45 km/s. The panel repeats the Astro-Volume controls: Δv slider with x0.1 and x10 buttons,
  and direction sliders in the prograde / normal / radial basis ("0 deg = prograde, 180 deg =
  retrograde"), plus LAUNCH.
- **Live readout** of the osculating orbit from `OrbitalElements.FromState`: a, e, i, periapsis,
  apoapsis, period, and ESCAPES / BOUND, plus the launch body's escape speed and the solar escape
  speed at the launch distance (the source's status line "Escape V: Earth = 11.2 km/s | Solar =
  ~42 km/s", now computed).
- **Time** is the global clock: warp speeds the probe up; reverse warp pauses the sandbox with a
  notice ("the sandbox integrates forward only").
- Up to 3 probes; trails as ring buffers with "10 s fade" or "Infinite" (the source's two modes);
  Clear probes.
- **Potential diagram** (off by default): the source's funnel grid was a fixed
  `z = -0.3/(d + 0.35)` unrelated to any mass ([HEL] §3.3); the port draws the depression from
  the real potential `Phi = -Sum GM_i / |r - r_i|`, labelled "DIAGRAM" (Procedural visual of an
  Inferred field).
- Tests (Core): energy conservation in a two-body case; a radial launch from Earth at 11.19 km/s
  escapes and at 11.0 does not; a Hohmann Earth-Mars transfer time within the vis-viva bound;
  forward-then-back integration returns within tolerance ([HEL] §7.7).

**Arcade slingshot** (TOOLS -> SANDBOX -> Arcade; a Fictional stage):

- The Orbital Slingshot game on a tabletop in the projector skin: five abstract planets with
  GM proportional to radius (so every planet has the same surface escape speed, the source's
  design lever, [HEL] §3.4), the pinch-and-pull impulse `min(|pull| · 4.8, 1.8)` m/s, surface

### 8.5 Room: passthrough and space

- One XR session with passthrough (V2's "both modes run in one session", [HEL] §2.8). TOOLS ->
  ROOM has a **room visibility** slider (100 % = passthrough, the default; 0 % = Space) and two
  presets, **Passthrough** and **Space**. Changes ease over 0.3 s (V2's `dt · 4`).
- Space draws an opaque backdrop that writes coverage (Foundation/Rendering own the mechanism),
  shows the `mwBand` layer, the CMB backdrop at the BEYOND rungs, the comfort floor ring, and a
  neutral translucent hand mesh so the user still sees their hands.
- **CMB backdrop in passthrough** (off by default) draws the CMB sky behind everything even with
  the room visible, for the user who wants it.
- Intermediate values dim the room behind the content, useful in a bright room.
- If passthrough is unavailable (permission denied), the app starts in Space and says so (§9.3).

### 8.6 Sizes and other honesty presets

| Preset | Radii | Moon orbits | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|
| **True** | true | true | true |
| **Readable** (default, today's) | `R_ref (R / R_ref)^0.55`, R_ref = Earth (VERIFIED [ARC] §2.6) | true | per the law |
| **x10 / x100 / x1000** | planets, moons and dwarfs x N | co-scaled about the primary by one factor per system, `min(N, 0.5 r_H / a_outermost)`, with the Hill radius `r_H = a (m / 3 M_sun)^(1/3)`, so the moons keep their order | capped at x25 (V2: "so it never swallows Mercury") |

- A moon whose drawn orbit falls inside its drawn primary is hidden and counted: chip
  `N moons hidden`. Example (INFERRED arithmetic): Mars's Hill radius is ~1.08e6 km, so its
  system factor caps at 23x (set by Deimos, a = 23,460 km); at x100 Phobos is drawn at 216,600 km,
  inside a 339,000 km Mars, and is hidden; Deimos, at 542,000 km, is not. This keeps V2's intent and fixes its
  x1000 absurdity of the Moon drawn outside Mars's orbit ([HEL] bug 5). V2's x1000 is kept because
  the planets themselves do not overlap at x1000 (Earth becomes 0.043 AU against 0.28 AU to Venus
  at closest, INFERRED arithmetic); only moons suffer, and they are counted.
- **RELIEF x1 / x10 / x50** (V2 and the Mars globe): elevation exaggeration on bodies with a
  shipped DEM (Data section: Moon LOLA, Mars MOLA candidates, [HEL] §6.4); disabled with "no
  elevation data for <body>" elsewhere; chip `RELIEF xN`.
- **The badge:** whenever the focus's `SizeExaggeration` exceeds 1.05, including in True mode when
  the 2.5 px minimum-size floor inflates a distant body, `SIZES NOT TO SCALE x<N>` (two
  significant figures) is drawn in Warning under the focus's label **in the headset** and as a
  header chip. Today it exists only in the desktop IMGUI HUD ([ARC] §2.6).
- The same chip family carries `BRIGHTNESS xN` and the other presentation gains (§4.4).

### 8.7 Specimen tray (Celestial Tray)

A room-anchored shelf, not part of the universe ([HEL] §7.13): **MODEL COPY: not the simulation**.

- Twelve specimens: Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth, Moon, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune,
  Pluto, Ceres, each with its real or procedural surface (with glyph) and its current IAU rotation.
- Size modes: **Equal** (3.5 cm radius each, the source's), **Readable** (the 0.55 law with Earth
  at 3.5 cm), **True** (Earth at 3.5 cm; the Sun would be 3.8 m in radius, VERIFIED arithmetic in
  [HEL] §7.13, so its slot says "Sun: 3.8 m radius at this scale, not shown").
- Pinch or grip a specimen: it grows to 0.22 m radius over 0.25 s and can be turned in the hand.
  Released within 0.2 m of its slot it docks and shrinks; released elsewhere it floats for 10 s,
  then returns. Grabbing the shelf's handle carries the whole tray.

### 8.8 Sound

- **Architecture** ([FIC] §4.5): DSP in `Core/Audio` (Kellet pink noise, oscillators, LFO, soft
  limiter; tests: pink slope -3 dB/octave within 0.5 dB (spectral-estimate noise over a 10 s
  sample, not the filter's own error, which is far smaller, INFERRED), the LFO never takes the cutoff below
  40 Hz, a chirp rises monotonically), one Runtime `CosmoAudio` component filling
  `OnAudioFilterRead`, followed by Unity's low-pass, echo and reverb filter components (their
  behaviour on Android is INFERRED; check on device). At most 8 voices.
- **Ambient bed** (default on, about -24 dBFS, INFERRED level): the synthesizer's "edge of the
  universe" preset, 65 Hz sine + 130 Hz triangle detuned +6 cents (the source never applied the
  detune, [FIC] §3.4 bug 1) + pink noise 0.4, low-pass 220 Hz Q 6 swept by a 0.04 Hz LFO kept
  within 60-600 Hz (the source's sweep sat at 0 Hz for ~30 % of each cycle, [FIC] §3.4 bug 4),
  delay 0.65 s feedback 0.75 mix 0.45, reverb ~6.5 s mix 0.65. The filter centre rises one octave
  per six rungs outwards. Legend: "ambient, not data".
- **Data voices** (default on), each with the provenance of what drives it: the selected
  **pulsar** as a click train at its spin frequency (Crab 29.6 Hz, Measured, [FIC] §3.2; others if
  the Data section ships a pulsar list); the **merger exhibit's** strain sent to audio
  (illustrative); **star voices** (off by default): the five brightest stars in view, temperature
  to timbre and flux to loudness (an authored convention, labelled); tapping the **CMB** plays a
  tone whose pitch follows dT (authored, labelled).
- **UI sounds:** hover 1500 Hz 30 ms at 0.045, press 880 -> 620 Hz 70 ms at 0.11 (atlas values),
  generated, not shipped.
- **Sound lab** (TOOLS -> SOUND -> Sound lab): the bed's whole chain with every parameter of the
  synthesizer's modules as sliders (VCO 1 and 2: frequency 40-800 Hz, fine ±50 cents, waveform;
  FM depth; noise level; VCF cutoff 60-5000 Hz, Q 0.5-20, modulation depth; LFO rate 0.02-15 Hz,
  depth; delay time 0.05-2 s, feedback 0-0.95, mix; reverb decay 0.5-8 s, mix; master volume), a
  scope strip, and Reset to preset. Mute persists between sessions.

### 8.9 View tools

- **Clean view** (the Vitals page's "Ambient"): hides labels, guides, cards and the panel; keeps
  a small `!` badge if sizes are not true. Any input brings the panel back.
- **Surface probe** (from the Mars globe's laser "GPS"): while the ray is on a drawn body, the card
  shows planetocentric latitude and longitude of the hit, and elevation where a DEM is shipped.
- **Free tilt**, **Smooth turn**, **Handedness** (§6.4, §6.5).
- **Quality:** Auto (the governor), Battery (72 Hz budgets), Max (90 Hz if it holds); the current

- **Recentre:** re-places the active stage 0.7 m ahead of the eyes (horizontally) and 0.25 m
  below eye height, facing the user. This is also where every rung frames its centre.

### 11.2 Build order (product view)

   `WarpLadder`, `TravelPlan` in Core with tests (no rendering needed).
2. `WristPanel` with GO / LAYERS / TIME on today's content, touch and ray input, and the binding
   changes; `TravelController`; the first run. This is the part the user has complained about since
   1.6.0 ("ugly, nonsense controls", `docs/feedback.md`).

   adding its row to the table.
4. Stages (Fictional, Phenomena), tools, sound.
